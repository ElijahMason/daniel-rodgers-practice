Sheet 02

A prototype answers a question.

Daniel Rodgers builds the first physical version of a mechanism or a circuit. The build has a job: settle something a drawing alone keeps fuzzy. When the question has an answer, he stops and writes what a factory needs.

Concept models

A concept model is a form you can turn over. Two people can point at the same surface and disagree usefully. It does not have to carry load. It does have to show the arrangement: what moves, what stays, and what a hand touches.

He builds the model early, while the mechanism can still change. A precious model that nobody is allowed to mark up has already failed its job. The marks are the point. They show which idea survived the hand.

Mechanical prototypes

A mechanical prototype spends its time on motion, fit, and load. Will the latch clear the rib at full travel. Does the shaft bind when the bracket flexes. Which face takes the wear. He builds the feature that holds the doubt, and keeps the rest plain enough that the answer stays visible.

The hinge study on the index asks where a knuckle gap lives. A clip asks a different question: can a beam pass a rib and then stay. He picks the build that matches the doubt, instead of making a whole product look finished.

Electrical prototypes

An electrical prototype is a circuit you can power and probe. He builds it when the schematic is still a claim. Values move. A connector turns out to sit in the way. A return path was a wish. The board that leaves the bench matches the schematic he writes after the probing, not the sketch from the night before.

The study below is only a topology: a source, a switch, a resistor, and a load, in series. It shows the kind of drawing a conversation can start from. It is not a board from a past job, and it is not a product.

Printed studies

A printed study is a fast way to hold a shape: a hinge, a clip, a housing split, the place a finger finds a switch. Plastic answers questions about geometry and assembly. It does not answer creep, heat, or a thread that must hold. He writes those limits on the sheet, and he names the production material even when the study was printed in something else.

The printed object is evidence for a drawing. A batch for sale is a different trade. When the shape has taught what it can, he stops printing and writes the handoff.
