Materials, then the line a factory can hold.

Daniel Rodgers makes the first physical version of a mechanism or a circuit. He learns from that version. He leaves drawings and files a factory can follow.

Make one that a factory can read.

The work is concept models, mechanical prototypes, electrical prototypes, printed studies, and the design-for-manufacture handoff. A printed study answers a question about shape or fit. He writes the production material on its own line. A print run, a software product, and a finished object for sale are other trades.

A prototype is a question with weight and a temperature.

Two essays follow. One is about choosing a material on purpose. One is about which lines on a drawing deserve a tolerance, and which lines should stay open so a machinist or a board house can work. The line drawings after them are studies: a hinge, a clip, a circuit. They are principles, not parts from a job.

The number on a real part comes after the part has taught it.
